3 Phone Changes Hitting Trade Shops Before Christmas 2026

Three things are about to hit your phone in the next six weeks, and none of them will wait for you to be ready. On 25 November 2026, the FCC’s rewritten TCPA opt-out rules take effect. On 27 November, Brown Friday — the busiest plumbing day of the year — lands on a holiday Friday when most small shops are closed or running a skeleton crew. And behind both of them, Google is moving Local Services Ads into Google Ads, on top of a rule that has charged you for missed calls since 1 October.

Each change on its own is manageable. Together, they make November the month to get your phone setup right — because from here on, a missed call costs you twice: once in the job you lose, and once in the lead fee you pay for it anyway.

This post covers what each change actually says, what it means for a plumbing, HVAC, electrical or roofing shop, and the short checklist to work through this week.

1. The FCC’s new TCPA opt-out rules take effect on 25 November 2026

On 30 September 2026 the Federal Communications Commission adopted an order, FCC 26-67, that rewrites the rules on how customers withdraw consent for automated calls and texts. It was published in the Federal Register on 26 October 2026 and takes effect on 25 November 2026 — the day before Thanksgiving. The FCC declined a request to delay it by up to 12 months, and as of 3 November 2026 no stay or court challenge had been found.

What actually changes

The “revoke all” rule is narrowed before it ever takes effect. The 2024 order would have required a business to treat an opt-out made in response to one type of message as applying to all future robocalls and robotexts from that caller. That version was delayed twice, most recently to 31 January 2027. The new order replaces it. Now, if a customer opts out of an informational message, you may treat that as covering only that category of informational message. If they opt out of a marketing message, it still ends all marketing from you.

You can name an exclusive opt-out method. A business may now designate one or more specific ways for customers to opt out — an automated key-press on a call, a reply text using a standardised keyword, or a website or phone number — provided the method is clearly and conspicuously disclosed on each call or text. If you do not name one, you must honour revocation by any reasonable means, including a free-form reply such as “leave me alone” or a verbal “take me off your list”.

One word per text is enough for the disclosure. If reply texting is your chosen opt-out method, each text can show a single standardised word, such as “Reply STOP to opt out”. You must still honour all seven standardised words: STOP, QUIT, END, REVOKE, OPT OUT, CANCEL and UNSUBSCRIBE.

The deadline stays at ten business days — for now. Opt-outs must still be honoured within a reasonable time, no later than ten business days. The FCC is separately asking whether to shorten that to seven business days, require two-way texting, and introduce a one-step “revoke all” option. Comments on those proposals are due 25 November 2026, with replies due 28 December 2026.

What a trade shop should do before 25 November

  • Check your missed-call text. If it carries no opt-out instruction, add one — “Reply STOP to opt out” is the simplest compliant form.
  • Make sure your tool honours all seven words, not just STOP. Most text-back tools do this automatically; check yours rather than assuming.
  • Keep the opt-out record. The guides stress what the consent record must show: the number, the scope, and where the opt-in came from.
  • Remember the quiet hours. The federal TCPA window is 8 a.m. to 9 p.m. in the recipient’s local time, not yours — and some states add their own rules on top.

2. Brown Friday, 27 November: the busiest plumbing day of the year

Thanksgiving falls on Thursday 26 November 2026. The next day, Friday 27 November, is what plumbers call Brown Friday — and Roto-Rooter calls it the busiest day of the year.

The numbers come from Roto-Rooter’s own historical service data, so treat them as a sign of the trend, not a promise for your shop: incoming service calls jump about 50% above an average Friday, and the four-day Thanksgiving weekend runs about 21% above other Thursday-to-Sunday stretches. A Dallas master plumber quoted on 10 November 2026 called it “the single busiest day of the year for residential drainage and sewer line issues”, with realtors and plumbers reporting a 20–50% spike in drain-cleaning and sewer-backup requests.

The top three problems, in Roto-Rooter’s order: kitchen-sink clogs and jammed garbage disposals, main sewer clogs, then toilet clogs. The causes are grease and hot turkey drippings, starches such as rice and pasta, and poultry skin and bones — plus a house full of guests, extra showers and marathon kitchen sessions.

The sting is the timing. The rush lands on a holiday Friday when many independent shops are closed or running a skeleton crew, and the first calls tend to arrive in a tight cluster before 9 a.m., almost all of them opening with the kitchen sink. A missed call that morning is not a call you get back later. The caller is standing in a kitchen with guests arriving and a sink that will not drain. They dial the next plumber on Google.

What to set up before the 27th

  • Decide who answers the phone on Brown Friday — and what happens when nobody can. If the shop is closed, a missed-call text-back is the difference between a caller who waits and a caller who moves on.
  • Write the Brown Friday text now. It should name the business, say when you will call back, and ask the one question that sorts the job: is water running where it should not be, or is it a slow drain that can wait until morning?
  • Do not promise a time you cannot keep. The honest version — “We’re booked solid today, first slot tomorrow at 8 a.m.” — keeps more jobs than a promise you break.
  • Put the safety line first for gas and sewage. If a caller smells gas, the first thing they hear should be: get everyone out and call 911 or the gas company from outside.

3. Google LSA is moving into Google Ads — and missed calls already cost you

The third change is the one already charging you. Since 1 October 2026, Google Local Services Ads can bill a business-hours call nobody answered if the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds. A call that goes to voicemail or an automated greeting counts as “connected” and is charged on the same rule, whether or not the caller leaves a message. Calls outside your stated business hours are not swept in — but “business hours” means your Google Business Profile hours, and if you also run an ad schedule, unanswered calls are only charged when they land inside both.

Three pieces of fine print matter:

  • One customer, one charge — for 15 days. Follow-up calls or messages from the same number or email are not charged again as long as there is contact within 15 days.
  • Credits are automatic, not on request. Google says it reassesses charged leads for 30 days and automatically credits ones it decides are poor quality, duplicate or invalid.
  • Answering every call will not lower your LSA bill. Answered calls were always judged on the conversation, not on ring time. The new cost is specifically the calls nobody answers.

And the platform itself is moving. Google is migrating Local Services Ads into specialised Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals inside Google Ads. Select US home-service advertisers — plumbing, HVAC, electrical, appliance repair, house cleaning, lawn care, roofing, pest control and moving — are in the first phase, which started around August 2026 and continues into 2027. You still pay per valid lead, not per click, but the campaign now lives inside Google Ads rather than as a standalone LSA product.

Put together, the message is blunt: the calls you miss are now the calls you pay for. In one audited sample, 27% of calls over 20 seconds never reached a real person even though the dialer marked them “Answered”. A missed-call text-back does not make the lead free — but it turns a paid-for ring into a conversation you can still win, instead of a fee and a lost job.

The one-week checklist

Work through this before 25 November and you are covered for all three changes:

  1. Add “Reply STOP to opt out” to your missed-call text, and confirm your tool honours all seven opt-out words (STOP, QUIT, END, REVOKE, OPT OUT, CANCEL, UNSUBSCRIBE).
  2. Check your Google Business Profile hours and your LSA ad schedule match the hours someone actually answers. Every hour listed as open but unanswered is an hour of missed calls you now pay for.
  3. Write your Brown Friday text and your after-hours text now, not on the 27th. Emergency or can-it-wait, a callback time you can keep, and the safety line first.
  4. Test the whole chain once. Ring your own number, let it ring out, and watch the text arrive. Check it comes from the number customers already call.
  5. Watch the carrier fees. A2P 10DLC surcharges rose again in 2026 — Verizon’s outbound SMS fee is $0.0050 per segment since 1 October, and the blended carrier fee is up about 30% on the year. The text-back still costs pennies; the missed call costs the job.

The bottom line

The FCC rule changes what your texts must say. Brown Friday changes how many calls arrive at once. Google’s LSA changes mean the calls you miss now show up on the invoice. None of the three is a reason to panic — but all three reward the same preparation: a text-back that fires in seconds, an opt-out line that is already compliant, and hours on Google that match the hours someone actually picks up.

Frequently asked questions

When do the FCC's new TCPA opt-out rules take effect?

25 November 2026. The order (FCC 26-67) was adopted on 30 September 2026 and published in the Federal Register on 26 October 2026. The FCC declined to delay it.

What is Brown Friday for plumbers?

The Friday after Thanksgiving — 27 November 2026 — when plumbing calls spike from kitchen-sink clogs, jammed garbage disposals, sewer backups and toilets after the big meal. Roto-Rooter reports calls about 50% above an average Friday, and calls it its busiest day of the year.

Does Google charge for missed calls on Local Services Ads?

Since 1 October 2026, yes — if the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds during your business hours, Google can count it as a valid, billable lead, even though nobody answered. A call to voicemail counts the same way.

Is Google moving Local Services Ads into Google Ads?

Yes. LSA is migrating into specialised Performance Max pay-per-lead campaigns inside Google Ads. The first US wave, including home services, started around August 2026 and continues into 2027. You still pay per valid lead, not per click.

What should a missed-call text say to stay compliant?
